1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW
================================================================================
During preparation for the 25-minute seminar presentation on DeepMind's AlphaFold 2
(Jumper et al., Nature 2021), a technical debate and team meeting took place
regarding both advanced biological/computational nuances (stoichiometry, contact
co-evolution, haem cofactors, MSA clustering) and the pedagogical framing of the
presentation deck.

The consensus reached was that the previous presentation structure treated key
mathematical concepts—specifically Invariant Point Attention (IPA) and the Frame
Aligned Point Error (FAPE)—too much as isolated, standalone mathematical lemmas,
disconnected from the actual neural network architecture. 

To fix this:
1. The architectural flow was grounded directly in the paper's Supplementary Figures.
2. Evita's Slide 8 was rebuilt around Supplementary Figure 2 (SI p. 16) to show
   the exact MSA row-wise gated self-attention mechanism and pair bias injection.
3. Ben's Slide 11 was rebuilt around Supplementary Figure 8 (SI p. 28) to show
   the three parallel affinity streams and the local pullback operator.
4. Slide 12 was re-anchored as the Structure Module's end-to-end supervision loop.
5. All detailed algebraic proofs (SE(3) group invariance, metric bounds, non-linear
   ablation dynamics) were preserved in Backup Slides 1–5 for targeted Q&A defense.

2. TECHNICAL INQUIRIES & CONCEPTUAL FOUNDATIONS
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Stoichiometry & Multimer Assemblies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Question: How does stoichiometry work in protein modeling, and how does AlphaFold
  handle multimeric complexes?
- Discussion:
  * Stoichiometry describes the exact integer molar ratios of distinct polypeptide
    chains in a biological complex (e.g., A2B2 for human hemoglobin with 2 alpha
    and 2 beta globin subunits; A4 for a homotetramer).
  * Monomeric AlphaFold 2 (Nature 2021) was trained purely on single polypeptide
    chains from the PDB. It used an artificial residue index offset (e.g., +1000)
    if users concatenated multiple chains into a single input, which lacked proper
    cross-chain relative positional encodings.
  * AlphaFold-Multimer (Evans et al., 2022) solved this natively by accepting user-
    specified stoichiometry (specifying exact copy counts of each chain).
  * The pair representation was partitioned into intra-chain blocks (residues
    within the same chain) and inter-chain blocks (residues between different chains),
    introducing symmetric positional encodings and chain identifier embeddings so
    the model distinguishes within-subunit distances from cross-subunit distances.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 Homotypic vs. Heterotypic Contacts & MSA Pairing Mechanics
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Question: What is the difference between homotypic and heterotypic contacts,
  and why does MSA pairing perform strongly on heterotypic contacts but weakly
  on homotypic contacts?
- Discussion:
  * Heterotypic contacts: Physical interfaces formed between two *different*
    proteins (e.g., Protein A and Protein B).
  * Homotypic contacts: Physical interfaces formed between two *identical* copies
    of the *same* protein (e.g., Monomer A1 and Monomer A2 in a homodimer).
  * Why heterotypic MSA pairing works well:
    - To detect co-evolution between Protein A and Protein B, one must align rows
      from the same organism (species matching) or from the same operon/genomic
      locus.
    - If residue i on Protein A mutates, and Protein B in the same organism undergoes
      a compensatory mutation at residue j, the co-variation across paired species
      is clean and unambiguous.
  * Why homotypic MSA pairing struggles (The Self-Interaction Ambiguity):
    - In a homomer, the sequences are identical! Residue i on chain A1 has the
      exact same sequence identity and evolutionary history as residue i on chain A2.
    - An intra-chain evolutionary signal between residue i and residue j within a
      single monomer cannot be cleanly mathematically distinguished from an
      inter-chain contact between residue i on chain A1 and residue j on chain A2.
    - Furthermore, homomers often exhibit cyclic (Cn) or dihedral (Dn) point-group
      symmetry where interface mutations can simultaneously affect multiple
      symmetric contact points, confounding pairwise correlation analysis.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3 Prosthetic Groups, Cofactors & Small Molecules (e.g., Haem Group)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Question: How does AlphaFold handle non-protein prosthetic groups like the Haem
  group in hemoglobin or myoglobin?
- Discussion:
  * AlphaFold 2 does NOT explicitly represent or output coordinates for small
    molecules, prosthetic groups (haem, flavin, ATP), metal ions (Fe, Zn, Mg),
    or post-translational modifications (glycosylation, phosphorylation).
  * Despite omitting haem from its inputs, AlphaFold 2 consistently predicts the
    empty haem-binding pocket with remarkable geometric precision (leaving the
    exact cavity open where the iron-porphyrin ring fits).
  * Reason: Millions of years of evolution have selected the surrounding amino
    acid backbone to hold that binding pocket rigid. The MSA contains strong
    conservation profiles for the proximal and distal histidines that coordinate
    the iron.
  * Generative Shift to AlphaFold 3 (2024): AF3 completely redesigned the generative
    pipeline, replacing the Structure Module with a full-atom Diffusion Module
    that natively models proteins, DNA, RNA, ligands, haem groups, and ions
    simultaneously in a single unified framework.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4 MSA Clustering & Representation Sampling
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Question: How does AlphaFold cluster and sample the MSA?
- Discussion:
  * Full genomic MSAs can contain hundreds of thousands of sequences (e.g., 200,000
    homologs). An attention mechanism over all sequences is computationally
    intractable (O(S^2 * R) memory).
  * AlphaFold 2 uses a two-tier MSA strategy:
    1. Cluster MSA Representation (m_si):
       - MSA sequences are grouped into clusters based on sequence identity.
       - A set of N_seq = 512 cluster representatives (centroids) are selected.
         The first row is always the query target sequence.
       - Each cluster representative is augmented with cluster profile statistics
         (average amino acid frequencies and deletion rates across all members
         assigned to that cluster).
       - Full row-wise and column-wise gated axial attention executes on this
         representation inside the 48 Evoformer blocks.
    2. Extra MSA Representation (e_si):
       - A larger sample of N_extra = 1024 (or up to 5120) raw sequences is kept.
       - These sequences do not pass through expensive axial self-attention;
         instead, they pass through simplified 4-block Extra MSA blocks that
         refine the pair matrix via Outer Product Mean projections at low cost.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.5 Biological Meaning of Homotypic vs. Heterotypic Underperformance
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Question: What does homotypic underperformance mean biologically?
- Discussion:
  * When a model struggles with homotypic assemblies compared to heterotypic ones,
    it reveals that the network cannot lean entirely on statistical co-evolution.
  * Biologically, homodimers rely heavily on structural symmetry constraints,
    hydrophobic exclusion at packing cores, and backbone rigidity rather than
    independent residue-pair co-adaptation.
  * For machine learning in structural biology, this demonstrates why pure sequence
    transformers fall short unless combined with explicit 3D inductive biases:
    when evolutionary signals become ambiguous (as in homomers), the network must
    rely on physical geometric reasoning (triangle consistency in 2D and rigid-body
    docking in 3D).

3. PRESENTATION RESTRUCTURING STRATEGY & TEAM CONSENSUS
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 The Meeting Critique: Disconnected Mathematical Formalisms
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In the seminar preparation meeting, the team agreed that:
- Previously, the presentations of Invariant Point Attention (IPA) and the Frame
  Aligned Point Error (FAPE) were introduced almost as abstract mathematical
  curiosities (equations and generic triad sketches).
- Audience feedback indicated that while the math was impressive, listeners lost
  track of *where* these operations lived inside the neural network and *why* the
  network needed them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 Evita's Strategy: Supplementary Figures 2 & 8 as Architectural Anchors
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evita and the team decided to ground the slides directly in DeepMind's official
system architecture schematics from the Supplementary Information:
- Slide 8: Use **Supplementary Figure 2** (SI p. 16, Algorithm 7) to visualize
  the exact row-wise gated self-attention block where sequence and pair tracks meet.
- Slide 11: Use **Supplementary Figure 8** (SI p. 28, Algorithm 22) to visualize
  the internal wiring of the Invariant Point Attention (IPA) module.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3 Pedagogical Hierarchy: Model Intuition vs. Backup Defense Proofs
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To satisfy both general clarity and high-level academic rigor:
- **Main 25-Minute Deck (Slides 1–20)**: Focuses on architectural dataflow,
  biological problem formulation, and physical constraints (invariance to rotations,
  triangle inequality closure, enantiomer chirality, loss landscape stability).
- **Backup Slides 1–5 (Q&A Defense)**: Reserved for formal mathematical derivations:
  * Backup 1: Rigorous algebraic proof of SE(3) invariance for IPA attention logits
    and SE(3) equivariance for local coordinate updates.
  * Backup 2: Metric proofs of FAPE on the quotient manifold R^(3M) / SE(3).
  * Backup 3: Triangle update derivations and Cauchy-Schwarz bounds.
  * Backup 4: Non-linear component ablation dynamics (-16.3 collapse).
  * Backup 5: Evolution from AlphaFold 2 to AlphaFold 3 generative diffusion.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4 Visual Design System: Red Callout Rectangles and Floating Badges
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Rather than displaying complex SI diagrams passively, prominent red rounded
callout rectangles (`\draw[callout]`) and floating white badges (`fill=white,
rounded corners=2pt`) were placed on the exact operational nodes:
- Supplementary Figure 2:
  * Pair bias box at the bottom.
  * The summation node (+) where pair spatial bias enters sequence logits.
  * The sigmoid gating module at the top right.
- Supplementary Figure 8:
  * Stream 1: Pair bias.
  * Stream 2: Scalar attention.
  * Stream 3: 3D coordinate point distance penalty.
  * Central summation node (+) before softmax.
  * The `invert` pullback node ($T_i^{-1}$) ensuring SE(3) equivariance.
