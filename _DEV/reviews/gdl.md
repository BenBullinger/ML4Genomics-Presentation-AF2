GDL reviewer: technical substance of the AlphaFold 2 deck
Checked against main.tex, SYMMETRY.tex, _AF2.pdf, _AF2-SI.pdf and AF3.pdf (main text only, the AF3 supplement is not in the repository). Rendered slides viewed at 1386 px. SI pages are printed pages.

1. Verdict

The AlphaFold 2 geometry on backups 1 to 4 is correct, sourced equation by equation, and the chirality mechanism on backup 3 is exactly right, which is rare in talks on this paper. The deck loses that credit in three places: backup 5 misreads the triangle updates and says geometry enters only through distances, backup 2 says reflections are "excluded on purpose" although the update rule is reflection-equivariant and the hand is broken elsewhere, and the AlphaFold 3 slides treat ligand stereocentres as the return of the mirror-image problem and "augmentation plus an aligned loss" as "symmetry dropped". Would it persuade me of research-level understanding of the geometry: not yet, but with the five fixes below, yes, and the SYMMETRY note already shows that the presenters know the right answers.

2. The five costliest weaknesses, ranked

(1) The spine credits the rotation symmetry to the frames and IPA, and reads the No-IPA ablation as the cost of that symmetry (slide 6 row 3, slide 18, slide 26, slide 23). In AlphaFold 2 the symmetry is carried by three things: the trunk sees only invariants (distances, torsions), the loss is invariant in local frames, and the structure module starts from identity frames and updates in local coordinates. The No-IPA ablation keeps the first two and replaces the module by a linear projection to a frame per residue (SI 1.13.1), so the 2.3 GDT measures iterative geometric refinement, not symmetry. The SI says so itself: "equivariance in the network is not essential" (SI p. 51). Fix: row 3 of slide 6 becomes "The network sees only invariants: distances in the trunk, local coordinates in the structure module, a loss in local frames", slide 18 labels the No-IPA bar as "refinement", and slide 23 says that AlphaFold 2's own ablation already contained AlphaFold 3's verdict (AF3 p. 495 quotes it: "removing most of the complexity of the structure module had only a modest effect"). This makes the story stronger, not weaker.

(2) Backup 5 is the slide a geometric deep learning group reads most carefully and it is the loosest. The triangle message is written as ψ(z_uw, z_wv), a path u to w to v. No update in the paper is that path: outgoing multiplication uses z_uw and z_vw (Algorithm 11, sum over k of a_ik b_jk), incoming uses z_wu and z_wv (Algorithm 12), start-node attention uses the query z_uv, keys and values z_uw and the bias z_vw (Algorithm 13), end-node the mirror of that (Algorithm 14). Because z_uv and z_vu differ, the direction matters. The key line "Geometry enters only through distances" contradicts the table's own IPA row ("values moved by T_u^-1 ∘ T_v") and the SI, which says IPA can distinguish reflections (SI p. 35), which distances cannot. Fix: ψ(z_uv, z_uw, z_vw) and ψ(z_uv, z_wu, z_wv) "over every third node w, two edges of each triangle into the third", and "Geometry enters only through invariants: distances in the weights, relative frames T_u^-1 ∘ T_v in the values".

(3) Backup 2's key line "Exact SE(3) equivariance. Reflections are excluded on purpose." As a map on frames the structure module is equivariant under all of E(3): the proof uses only ‖R x‖ = ‖x‖ and associativity, both of which hold for det R = −1. SYMMETRY.tex section 2 says exactly this ("IPA as a function of frames is invariant under all of E(3). Chirality is not in IPA"). The SI sentence on p. 28 only defines the word "rigid motion", it does not claim that the module breaks mirror symmetry. The mirror symmetry is broken where frames are built from atoms (Algorithm 21, eq. 11), in the loss, and in the side-chain construction, which is what backup 3 then shows. Fix: "Exact SE(3) equivariance by construction. The same algebra holds for reflections of the frames. The hand is broken elsewhere: backup 3."

(4) The AlphaFold 3 framing overstates in two ways (slides 23, 25, 28). First, "The hand came back": AlphaFold 2's FAPE fixes the global mirror image of a fold (Suppl. Fig. 9). AlphaFold 3's 4.4 % is the rate of wrong chiral centres of ligands on PoseBusters after a ranking penalty (AF3 p. 497, Fig. 5b: alpha instead of beta glucuronic acid), a failure AlphaFold 2 could not have because it never built ligands and built side chains with ideal geometry, and AlphaFold 3 receives the chirality as an input feature (reference conformers, p. 497). Second, "AlphaFold 3 dropped both and learns them from data" (slide 28): AlphaFold 3's trunk is still invariant by construction (distances), its diffusion noise is isotropic, and its loss aligns the truth to the prediction. What was dropped is the exact equivariance of the decoder, replaced by random rotations and translations (Fig. 2b) and the alignment in the loss. Fix: "The hand came back in a different place: at chiral centres of ligands, not as a mirrored fold", and on slide 28 "replaced the equivariant decoder by a diffusion model trained with random rotations and a loss that aligns first".

(5) Slide 26 states "trained end to end, in about one GPU minute per model". One GPU minute is inference per model for 384 residues (paper p. 588); training takes about eleven days on 128 TPU v3 cores (backup 9). On the same slide "frames and IPA −2.3" is wrong in the detail (the ablation keeps the frames) and "triangles on the pairs −4.6" bundles triangles, the pair bias into the MSA and gating (SI 1.13.1). Fix: "Trained end to end. A prediction takes about one GPU minute for 384 residues", "IPA and iterative refinement −2.3", "triangles, pair bias and gating −4.6".

What a careful equivariance researcher would add on "random rotations instead of equivariance", and what the deck should not claim: augmentation gives approximate equivariance in expectation and is exact only in the limit of data, and AlphaFold 3 reports no test of orientation dependence and no ablation of the removed parts, so the evidence is "at this scale and data size augmentation sufficed", not "equivariance was a mistake". The comparison is confounded, since trunk, data, loss, decoder and sampling changed at once. The usual argument for equivariance is sample efficiency and behaviour out of distribution, and nothing in AF3 speaks to it. AlphaFold 2's equivariance was never heavy: no spherical harmonics, no equivariant kernels, only points expressed in per-residue frames, which SYMMETRY.tex says well and the deck should say once. AlphaFold 3's own stated reasons are generality (ligands and nucleic acids have no residue frames or torsions) and simplicity (p. 495 to 496), not Sutton's lesson; "the bitter lesson" should be marked as the presenters' reading. The triangles, a hand-designed inductive bias, survived, which the deck rightly keeps as a footnote.

3. Per-slide findings

Severity: high = wrong, medium = imprecise or overstated, low = terminology or presentation. "Verified" marks derivations I re-derived myself.

Slide 6. Medium. Row 3's "Attention uses only distances in the frames" is true for the weights only. The value points are read back in the local frame (Algorithm 22 line 10), and that is the part of IPA that can see the hand. The answer to fact 3 in AlphaFold 2 is invariants everywhere plus an invariant loss, not IPA alone (weakness 1). Row 2's "Distances in 3D must close triangles" is a necessary condition only. The paper says "many constraints must be satisfied including the triangle inequality" (p. 585 to 586). Fix: reword row 3 as in weakness 1. Optional one clause that would show the GDL view: fact 1 is a permutation symmetry of the input, fact 3 a symmetry of the output, fact 4 a symmetry that must not hold.

Slide 9. Low. "Templates and the previous cycle enter as distances." Templates enter the pairs as binned Cβ distances (the unit-vector feature is set to zero, SI Table of features) and the MSA rows as torsion angles (template_angle_feat), and torsion angles change sign under reflection, so the trunk is not fully blind to the hand when templates exist. The previous cycle also feeds back the pair representation itself (Algorithm 32 line 3). Fix: "Templates enter the pairs as Cβ distances and the MSA as torsion angles. The previous cycle enters as Cβ distances plus the previous pair representation."

Slide 10. Medium. "The edges are residues in proximity" suggests a sparse graph. The pair representation is a complete directed graph on residues with 128 numbers per edge, and proximity is what the edges must learn to encode. In GDL terms the inductive bias is not in the connectivity, it is in the triangle updates and in the factorisation of attention along rows and columns. Fix: "A complete graph: every ordered pair is an edge. In proximity is what the network must infer." The phrase "graph inference" is the paper's (p. 585) and can stay or go, it adds little.

Slide 13. Medium. "The triangle inequality acts as an inductive bias." Nothing computes or enforces the triangle inequality. The paper says the triangle arrangement is inspired by the need for consistency, the triangle inequality among it, and the SI calls it "the triangle inductive bias" (SI p. 48). Fix: "Updates are arranged in triangles so that the network can learn 3D consistency. The paper's intuition is the triangle inequality. Nothing is enforced." The edge directions on this slide (ik and jk) match Algorithms 11 and 13. The ablation number for this row (−4.6) removes three things, see slide 26.

Slide 14. Low. The title says rigid bodies, the text says frames. Say once that a residue is a rigid body and its pose is the frame (the paper's residue gas, p. 586). The word "equivariant" never appears on a main slide, yet slides 23 and 28 lean on it. The paper's own sentence would serve as the key line: "The application of these updates within the local frame of each residue makes the overall attention and update block an equivariant operation" (p. 587). Missing, and one line would do: the input has no geometry, so the symmetry acts on the output, and the loss must carry it.

Slide 15. Low. The formula is Algorithm 22 line 7 without w_L, 1/sqrt(c) and γ w_C / 2, as the footnote says. Correct. The slide never says that the values are read back in frame i, which is where IPA's local geometry lives. The paper's remark that the distance term imposes a strong spatial locality bias (p. 587) is worth one clause for this audience. Fix: add "values are read back in frame i".

Slide 16. Low. The formula matches Algorithm 28 up to ε. Correct. Index convention differs from slide 15 (i, j, p), backups 3 and 4 (T_i, x_j and k, i) and backup 5 (u, v, w). One convention for frames and atoms across the deck, with u, v only on backup 5.

Slide 18. Medium. The key line implies that each bar is the cost of the symmetry named on slide 6. For row 3 the ablation keeps the frames, FAPE and the invariant trunk and removes geometric attention and the eight refinement steps (SI 1.13.1). The SI says the single IPA ablation "removes all equivariant components" and is "still a very accurate structure prediction network", and that the double ablation "must be interpreted with caution" (SI p. 51). Row 2's bar removes triangles, the pair bias into the MSA and gating together. Row 4 ("mirror-blind loss") matches SI 1.9.3 ("frequently produces mirror structures"). Fix: footnote "No IPA keeps the frames and FAPE. It removes the geometric attention and the iteration."

Slide 23. Medium. Verified against AF3: smaller MSA block and MSA not retained (p. 494), pair processing and 48 blocks largely unchanged (p. 494), diffusion "without rotational frames or any equivariant processing" (p. 495), "no invariance or equivariance ... required ... we therefore omit them" (p. 496), random rotation and translation (Fig. 2b legend), 4.4 % chirality violation rate (p. 497). Two overstatements, see weakness 4: "The hand came back" conflates global handedness with ligand stereocentres, and the row "FAPE dropped" rests on the AF3 supplement (the diffusion loss, an aligned MSE), which is not in the repository and should be cited once verified. The bitter-lesson label is the presenters' reading, AF3 gives generality and simplicity as its reasons. Fix: "The hand came back in a different place: at chiral centres of ligands, not as a mirrored fold", and "our reading" before the Sutton line.

Slide 25. Low. Rows verified: chirality penalty in ranking and multiple seeds (p. 497), clash penalty in ranking reduces but does not eliminate (p. 497), cross-distillation from AlphaFold-Multimer v2.3 against hallucination plus a solvent-accessible-surface ranking term (p. 496 to 497), MSA dependence similar to AlphaFold-Multimer (p. 497), the one RNA exception AIchemy_RNA2 with human input (p. 497). "No ablation of the removed parts is reported" holds for the main text. "loops for disorder" was not something AlphaFold 2 had as a design choice, it was emergent. Fix: "ribbons for disorder (emergent)".

Slide 26. High. "Evolution and geometry are trained end to end, in about one GPU minute per model" reads as a training time. It is inference per model for 384 residues (p. 588). "frames and IPA −2.3": the ablation keeps the frames. "triangles on the pairs −4.6": bundles three removals. Fixes in weakness 5.

Slide 28. Medium. Question 1's premise "AlphaFold 3 dropped both and learns them from data" overstates, see weakness 4. Fix: "AlphaFold 3 replaced the equivariant decoder by a diffusion model trained with random rotations and a loss that aligns first, and gives chirality as an input. When is exact symmetry worth building in, and what experiment would show it?" Questions 2 and 3 are outside my brief.

Backup 1. Low. Verified. Both chains are the SI's equations 3 to 6 and are correct, and the slide marks them as the SI's. Two places where the slide could be more careful than the SI: T_global applied to a difference of two points is only the rotation R_global, written as on the slide (and in SI eq. 3) it adds a spurious translation. The second chain needs the weights to sum to one, which softmax guarantees, otherwise the translation does not pass through the sum. Fix: write R_global in the first chain, and add "the weights sum to one" to the grey line. Both cancellations use only orthogonality, which also holds for reflections, see backup 2.

Backup 2. High as a GDL audience reads it, see weakness 3. Verified: the associativity line is correct and the equivariance claim is the SI's (p. 28). "The output's global frame is arbitrary" should read "the output lives in the frame of the black-hole initialisation, unrelated to the experiment's frame". Fix as in weakness 3, plus the one clause "the symmetry acts on the output, so the loss must carry it".

Backup 3. Low. Verified: eq. 11 (e1 and e2 flip sign with the atoms, e3 = e1 × e2 does not, the translation flips), the z flip of local coordinates (eqs. 12 to 15) and FAPE(X, X_mirror) = 2 Σ |z| (eqs. 16 to 17). All three are the SI's and the slide cites them as such. Two imprecisions: the SI's equations 16 to 17 are written without the clamp, the mean and 1/Z, so "FAPE(X, X_mirror) = 2 Σ |z|" is not the FAPE of slide 16, with the clamp it is (1/Z) mean min(10 Å, 2|z|). Fix: "unclamped FAPE". "in a large share of cases": the SI says "frequently" and shows a bimodal GDT without a number. Also "A frame is built from N, Cα and C" holds for the ground-truth frames, predicted frames come from quaternions (det +1 as well). This is the best slide of the deck.

Backup 4. Medium. Verified. "FAPE is at least the RMSD after the best rigid alignment": eq. 18 defines S as the mean point error over the best proper alignment, an "RMSD-like" quantity in the SI's own words, not the RMSD. FAPE ≥ S (eqs. 19 to 22) is right, and S = 0 iff RMSD = 0 gives the zero set. "pseudometric" holds for idealised FAPE (ε = 0, SI 1.9.5), say so. The invariance claim is right and needs no sum-to-one condition. Missing and strongest: "FAPE is non-zero for chiral pairs of non-degenerate point sets regardless of how the frames are built" (SI p. 36), which makes the hand independent of Algorithm 21. Fix: "at least the mean point error after the best proper rigid alignment (eq. 18)".

Backup 5. High, see weakness 2. In addition: the formula is Bronstein's message-passing flavour with edge features, which covers node updates, while the outer product mean and the triangle rows are edge updates, which the formula does not cover, backup 6 admits this in its footnote, say "edge version" once. Row 1: the graph is complete, N_u is all residues. "A frame is a gauge fixed by its atoms" is a defensible analogy (a canonical local frame removes the gauge freedom, and the SI p. 34 says any consistent construction of frames works for FAPE), but it is stated as a definition, and predicted frames are not fixed by atoms, the atoms are built from them. Fix: "The frame plays the role of a gauge, chosen canonically from N, Cα, C (our reading)". "Triangles are two-hop messages" becomes "two edges of each triangle into the third".

Backup 6. Low. Correct as a table. Line 11 omits that the recycled pair representation also includes the previous pair representation (Algorithm 32 line 3). "Information moves in lines 1, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 11" should say "between residues", line 2 moves it between sequences. Duplicates the table of backup 5, keep one.

Backup 7. Low. "No triangles, biasing or gating" also replaces the pair bias in the MSA row attention, so the pairs no longer steer the MSA (SI 1.13.1), add it. The No-IPA row is right. The SI's own caution on the double ablation (it is unclear which missing feature of recycling explains it) would make a better grey line than "not re-tuned".

Skipped: slides 1 to 5, 7, 8, 11, 12, 17, 19 to 22, 24, 27 and backups 8 to 11 (source read, not geometry, no finding beyond backup 10's AF3 supplement citation, which I could not verify).

4. What is right and strong, do not change by accident

The four-fact spine with the four red discs is a clear and honest structure, and facts 1, 2 and 4 are right as stated. Slide 15's formula and its drawing (points placed in the frames, the dashed distances unchanged after a rotation) are exactly the SI's mechanism. Slide 16's formula is Algorithm 28. Backup 1's two cancellations, backup 2's associativity line, backup 3's eq. 11, the z flip and the dRMSD evidence, and backup 4's lower bound and pseudometric are correct and are cited to the SI equations, so the deck never claims the SI's derivations as its own. Backup 3's closing line "The loss sees the hand, the data teach it. Side chains are always built L." is the correct two-part answer. Slide 18 shows both test sets, so the audience can see that No IPA costs 0.3 lDDT on PDB chains. Slide 25's "No ablation of the removed parts is reported" is the right caveat. The SYMMETRY note is better than the slides on E(3) versus SE(3) and on what not to say (no spherical harmonics, no equivariant kernels), and its final paragraph should be the Q&A answer.
